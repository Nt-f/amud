## Siddur is now Amud
- **A new name and look.** The app is now called Amud (עמוד), with a new icon and a short animation when it opens. The website has moved to **amud.page**. The web app is at amud.page/app, and old links and home-screen installs are sent there.
- **Android: a new app.** Amud has a new app ID, so Android installs it next to the old Siddur app instead of updating it. Settings, alerts and your Home cards don't carry over. Once Amud is set up, you can uninstall Siddur.
- **More places to get it.** Amud is now released for Mac and Linux as well as Android, Windows and the web. There's also an unsigned iPhone build for sideloading. On iPhone, you can use the web app instead: open it in Safari and tap Share → Add to Home Screen.

## New
- **Torah tab.** The Calendar tab is now the Torah tab. Download the Kitzur Shulchan Aruch from Sefaria (about 1 MB) and learn it offline, in Hebrew, English or both. Today's Kitzur Yomi is marked, with a link to it from Halacha. Chumash, Gemara and more are on the way.
- **Separate text settings for learning.** The Torah tab has its own language, Hebrew font, English font, text size and nikud settings, separate from the siddur's.
- **Calendar card.** The calendar is now a card on Home, below Shabbat & Yom Tov and the next zman. Tap the expand button to open it full screen.
- **Today's davening.** The whole day's davening in order, from Shacharis to the bedtime Shema, with what's added today, such as Hallel, Musaf, Lulav or the day's Hoshana. Under each section, links lead to what comes next today and to related prayers. Open it from *Today in the siddur* on Home, or from the Siddur tab.
- **Holidays & Seasons.** Hoshanot, Lulav, Selichot, Chanukah candles, Hakafot and other holiday prayers in one place, from your nusach first, with the ones for today marked. Find it in the Siddur tab.
- **Prayer title language.** Show prayer titles in English, Hebrew or both, whatever the app language. It's under Settings → Siddur, or Text settings in the reader.

## Improved
- The parsha on the Hebrew date card skips holiday readings. On Shabbat Chol HaMoed it shows the next regular parsha and its date.
- The bold opening words of a prayer look the same whichever text version a section comes from.
- More of the app is translated into Hebrew and Yiddish, including zman alerts, custom zmanim, daily learning and custom cards. Dates and countdowns follow the app language.
- The Torah reading at Mincha is shown only on Shabbat, fast days and Yom Kippur.
- Each day's Hoshanot come before the general ones, so the right one shows.

## Fixed
- Text that's said only on Shabbat now also shows when the Shabbat service is used on Yom Tov.
